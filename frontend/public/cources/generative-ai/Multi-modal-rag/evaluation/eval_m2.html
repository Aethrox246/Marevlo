01 · The Reference-Free Advantage

Evaluating Quality Without
a Gold Standard Answer

Reference-based metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore) require a gold standard answer for every query. This is expensive — annotating 500 gold answers might take 40+ hours of expert time. Reference-free quality dimensions evaluate the response in isolation: is it internally consistent? Does it address the question? Is it grounded in its stated context? No annotation required.

Reference-based: what you need

✗ 500 expert-annotated gold answers = 40+ hours
✗ Gold answers become stale when knowledge base updates
✗ Multiple valid answers penalized (only one reference used)
✗ Open-ended queries (plan my trip) have no single correct answer

Reference-free: what you need

✓ Just the query + response (+ retrieved context for RAG)
✓ Scales to 100% of production traffic automatically
✓ Catches new failure modes without updating test set
✓ Works for open-ended, creative, and conversational responses
02 · Coherence

Does the Response Flow Logically?
Definition, Rubric, and Examples

Coherence measures whether the ideas in a response are logically connected, whether sentences follow from each other without contradiction, and whether the overall structure makes sense. A response can be fluent (grammatically correct) but incoherent (logically contradictory or disconnected).

Coherence scoring rubric (1–5)

Score 1: Sentences contradict each other or have no logical connection. Structure is incomprehensible.
Score 2: Major gaps in logic. Ideas loosely related but structure is hard to follow.
Score 3: Generally coherent with some unclear transitions or minor logical gaps.
Score 4: Coherent with clear logical flow. Minor organizational improvements possible.
Score 5: Perfectly coherent. Ideas flow logically. Clear structure. No contradictions.
Score 1 — Incoherent (contradicts itself)
Query: "What are the refund options?"

Response: "You can get a full refund within 30 days. Annual plans are non-refundable. However, all plans include a money-back guarantee. Refunds are not available for any reason after purchase."

❌ Claims: full refund available → annual non-refundable → all plans have money-back → no refunds available. Four contradictory statements.
Score 5 — Coherent
Query: "What are the refund options?"

Response: "Refund eligibility depends on your plan type. Monthly subscribers can cancel anytime with a prorated refund for unused days. Annual subscribers have a 30-day money-back window from their start date — after that, the subscription runs until the end of the billing year without a refund."

✓ Clear structure. Information is logically organized. No contradictions. Each sentence builds on the previous.
03 · Relevance

Does the Answer Address
the Actual Question?

Relevance measures whether the response answers what was asked — not whether it is accurate, and not whether it is coherent. A response can be perfectly coherent and factually accurate but completely irrelevant: it answers a different question than the one asked.

❌ Relevance = 1/5 — Accurate but off-topic
Query: "Can I pause my subscription?"

Response: "Our company was founded in 2018 with a mission to make quality software accessible. We offer monthly and annual plans. Our customer support team is available 24/7. We process refunds within 5-7 business days."

❌ All statements may be accurate. Zero statements address "can I pause my subscription?"
✓ Relevance = 5/5 — Directly answers the question
Query: "Can I pause my subscription?"

Response: "Yes, you can pause your subscription for up to 3 months. To pause, go to Account Settings → Subscription → Pause. Your billing stops immediately and resumes when you un-pause or when the 3-month limit is reached."

✓ Directly answers: yes/no + how long + how to do it. Zero off-topic content.
💡

Relevance ≠ Accuracy — the critical distinction

A response can be 100% accurate and 0% relevant. This happens when the retriever returns correct documents about the wrong topic, or when the LLM answers a question it found in the retrieved context rather than the user's actual question. Always measure relevance separately from faithfulness: a high-faithfulness, low-relevance system is accurately answering the wrong question.

04 · Faithfulness

Grounded Claims vs Hallucinated Claims:
Measuring What the Model Invents

Faithfulness measures the fraction of factual claims in the response that are supported by the provided context. A faithfulness of 0.75 means 25% of claims were hallucinated — invented by the model without evidence in the retrieved documents. This is the most critical metric for production RAG systems.

Faithfulness calculation: decomposing claims

Retrieved context: "Annual subscribers may request a refund within the first 30 calendar days of their subscription. Refund requests must be submitted via the account portal. Processing takes 5-7 business days."

Model response: "Annual plans have a 30-day refund window. You can submit via the portal. Refunds process in 5-7 days. Monthly plans are also refundable. Customer support can expedite the process."

faithfulness_scorer.py — NLI-based implementation

from transformers import pipeline
from langchain_anthropic import ChatAnthropic
from langchain_core.prompts import ChatPromptTemplate
from langchain_core.output_parsers import JsonOutputParser

# Method 1: NLI-based (fast, free, ~90% accuracy)
nli = pipeline("text-classification", model="cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-base")

def claim_is_entailed(claim: str, context: str) -> bool:
    """Check if claim is supported by context via NLI."""
    result = nli(f"{context} [SEP] {claim}")[0]
    return result["label"] == "ENTAILMENT"

# Method 2: LLM-based claim decomposition + verification (higher accuracy)
llm = ChatAnthropic(model="claude-haiku-4-5-20251001")  # use fast model

DECOMPOSE_PROMPT = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
    ("system", "Decompose this text into individual atomic claims.
Output JSON: {"claims": ["claim1", "claim2", ...]}"),
    ("human", "{response}")
])

VERIFY_PROMPT = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([
    ("system", "Does the context support this claim?
Output JSON: {"supported": true/false, "reason": "..."}
Context: {context}"),
    ("human", "Claim: {claim}")
])

decompose_chain = DECOMPOSE_PROMPT | llm | JsonOutputParser()
verify_chain    = VERIFY_PROMPT    | llm | JsonOutputParser()

def compute_faithfulness(response: str, context: str) -> dict:
    # Step 1: Decompose response into claims
    claims = decompose_chain.invoke({"response": response})["claims"]
    
    # Step 2: Verify each claim against context
    results = []
    for claim in claims:
        v = verify_chain.invoke({"context": context, "claim": claim})
        results.append({"claim": claim, **v})
    
    supported = sum(1 for r in results if r["supported"])
    return {
        "faithfulness": supported / len(claims) if claims else 1.0,
        "total_claims": len(claims),
        "hallucinated": len(claims) - supported,
        "per_claim": results
    }

# Example:
result = compute_faithfulness(
    response="Annual plans have a 30-day refund window. Monthly plans are also refundable.",
    context="Annual subscribers may request a refund within the first 30 calendar days."
)
# → {"faithfulness": 0.50, "total_claims": 2, "hallucinated": 1, ...}
05 · Groundedness

Faithfulness vs Groundedness:
The Critical Distinction

Faithfulness and groundedness are closely related but measure different things. Faithfulness asks: "Are the claims supported by context?" Groundedness asks: "Is the answer derived from and traceable to the provided context, or does it draw on training data?" A grounded answer cites specific passages. A faithful answer is consistent with context but may not cite it explicitly.

High faithfulness, low groundedness

Context: "Annual subscribers may request a refund within 30 calendar days."

Response: "Yes, you can get a refund." (Correct — consistent with context — but does not specify 30 days or cite source)
Faithfulness = 1.0 (no hallucination) | Groundedness = 0.4 (vague, no citation)

High faithfulness AND high groundedness

Context: "Annual subscribers may request a refund within 30 calendar days."

Response: "According to our refund policy, annual subscribers can request a refund within the first 30 calendar days of their subscription."
Faithfulness = 1.0 | Groundedness = 0.9 (specific, cites "according to refund policy")
06 · Completeness and Conciseness

Did It Answer Everything?
Did It Say Too Much?

Completeness and conciseness are the opposite poles of response scope. A complete response covers all aspects of the question without omitting critical information. A concise response removes unnecessary padding without losing substance. Ideally a response is both: complete and concise. In practice you often need to choose.

ScenarioCompletenessConcisenessWhat happened
Query: "How do I cancel and get a refund?" Response addresses only cancellation, not refund.2/5 — missing refund info5/5 — no paddingPartial answer. User must ask again for refund info.
Same query. Response covers cancellation, refund process, refund timeline, refund exceptions, refund for sub-accounts, billing cycle, and proration with examples.5/5 — complete2/5 — excessiveInformation overload. User buried in irrelevant details.
Same query. Response covers cancellation steps (3 sentences) + refund eligibility (2 sentences) + timeline (1 sentence).5/55/5Ideal: complete and concise.
Same query. Response: "Cancellation depends on your plan type."1/55/5No useful information despite being concise.
⚠️

LLM judges have a verbosity bias toward high completeness scores

LLM judges tend to rate longer responses as "more complete" even when the extra content is padding or tangential information. Mitigate this by: (1) using a rubric that explicitly defines completeness as "addresses all aspects of the question, not more" and (2) separately checking for padding with a conciseness question. Always score both dimensions independently, not as a single "thoroughness" score.

07 · Toxicity, Fluency, and Bias

Safety and Style Dimensions:
What Automated Tools Can Detect

Toxicity, fluency, and bias are typically measured by specialized models rather than LLM judges. Toxicity classifiers (Perspective API, Detoxify) flag harmful, offensive, or abusive language. Fluency models measure grammatical correctness. Bias evaluation tools (BBQ, WinoBias) test whether the system treats demographic groups equitably.

Toxicity — Perspective API

Returns probability scores (0-1) for: toxicity, severe_toxicity, identity_attack, insult, profanity, threat. Threshold typically 0.5+ = toxic.

from googleapiclient.discovery import build client = build("commentanalyzer", "v1alpha1", ...) result = client.comments().analyze(body={ "comment": {"text": response}, "requestedAttributes": { "TOXICITY": {}, "IDENTITY_ATTACK": {} }}).execute() toxicity = result["attributeScores"]["TOXICITY"]["summaryScore"]["value"] # flag if toxicity > 0.5

Bias — Demographic Parity Testing

Test whether the model responds differently to the same question when demographic details are swapped. A biased model answers "Is [male name] qualified?" differently than "Is [female name] qualified?" for identical credentials.

# Template pair testing templates = [ "Is {name} a good candidate for the engineer role?" ] male_names = ["John", "David", "Michael"] female_names = ["Sarah", "Emma", "Jessica"] # Score both, check for systematic difference # |avg_male_score - avg_female_score| > 0.1 = bias signal

Fluency — Grammar Checker or PPL

Fluency can be measured via grammar checkers (LanguageTool) or perplexity on a well-tuned language model. Unlike quality metrics, fluency is rarely the limiting factor for modern LLMs — they almost always produce grammatically correct text.

import language_tool_python tool = language_tool_python.LanguageTool("en-US") matches = tool.check(response) # grammar_errors = len(matches) # fluency_score = max(0, 1 - errors/len(words)) # Modern LLMs: typically 0-2 errors per response
08 · Scoring All 8 Dimensions with an LLM Judge

One Prompt to Score
All Quality Dimensions

multi_dimension_judge.py

from langchain_anthropic import ChatAnthropic
from langchain_core.prompts import ChatPromptTemplate
from langchain_core.output_parsers import JsonOutputParser

llm = ChatAnthropic(model="claude-sonnet-4-20250514", temperature=0)

JUDGE_PROMPT = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([("system", """
You are an expert evaluator for AI system outputs.
Score the following response on EACH dimension 1-5:

COHERENCE: Are ideas logically connected? No contradictions?
  1=contradicts itself, 5=perfectly logical flow
RELEVANCE: Does it answer the actual question asked?
  1=completely off-topic, 5=directly and fully addresses the question
FAITHFULNESS: Are all claims supported by the provided context?
  1=mostly hallucinated, 5=every claim traceable to context (only relevant for RAG)
COMPLETENESS: Does it cover all aspects of the question?
  1=major omissions, 5=fully addresses all aspects
CONCISENESS: Is it appropriately brief without unnecessary padding?
  1=extremely padded/redundant, 5=perfectly concise

Output ONLY valid JSON:
{{"coherence": <1-5>, "relevance": <1-5>, "faithfulness": <1-5>,
  "completeness": <1-5>, "conciseness": <1-5>,
  "rationale": {{"coherence": "...", "relevance": "...", "faithfulness": "...",
                 "completeness": "...", "conciseness": "..."}}}}

Context (if provided): {context}
"""),
("human", "Question: {question}
Response: {response}")])

judge_chain = JUDGE_PROMPT | llm | JsonOutputParser()

def evaluate_response(question: str, response: str, context: str = "N/A") -> dict:
    scores = judge_chain.invoke({
        "question": question,
        "response": response,
        "context": context
    })
    scores["overall"] = sum([
        scores["coherence"], scores["relevance"],
        scores["completeness"], scores["conciseness"]
    ]) / 4
    return scores

# Example evaluation
result = evaluate_response(
    question="Can I pause my subscription?",
    response="Our company offers monthly and annual plans. You can get a refund within 30 days.",
    context="Subscriptions can be paused for up to 3 months via Account Settings."
)
# Expected: coherence=4, relevance=1, faithfulness=1, completeness=1, conciseness=3
# Overall = 2.25 — fails relevance catastrophically
print(f"Overall quality: {result['overall']:.2f}/5")
print(f"Relevance: {result['relevance']}/5 — {result['rationale']['relevance']}")
09 · Assessment

Module 2 Quiz

1. A RAG system achieves Faithfulness = 0.95 on a test set. A new manager declares "our hallucination problem is solved." What critical information is missing before you can agree?

Correct. Faithfulness is conditional on the quality of the retrieved context. A faithfulness of 0.95 with wrong retrieved documents means the system confidently and consistently answers incorrectly — it is faithfully reproducing the errors in its context. You must also measure Context Precision (are the retrieved documents relevant?) to confirm the system is faithfully reproducing correct information.

2. You score a response on coherence = 4/5 and relevance = 1/5. What does this pattern tell you about the failure?

Correct. Coherence = 4 + Relevance = 1 is the "wrong topic, well written" pattern. The model is not confused or hallucinating — it has produced a coherent response. But it answered a question the user did not ask. The most common cause: the retriever returned documents that are topically adjacent to the query but answer a different question, and the LLM faithfully synthesized an answer from those documents without recognizing the topic mismatch.

3. You are scoring 10,000 production responses for completeness using an LLM judge. A colleague reports that long responses always score 5/5 on completeness regardless of whether they actually answer the question. What is happening and how do you fix it?

Correct. Verbosity bias is one of the most well-documented LLM judge failure modes. The judge learned from human preference data where humans often rated longer responses as "better" even when shorter responses were correct. The rubric fix is the most effective remedy: explicit guidance with negative examples ("A short answer that covers all aspects scores 5; a long answer with irrelevant padding scores 3 even if accurate") calibrates the judge against this systematic error.

4. You test your travel agent with identical queries but swap the customer name: "John is planning a trip to Tokyo" vs "Sarah is planning a trip to Tokyo." The agent's hotel recommendations differ significantly. What evaluation dimension is failing?

Correct. Demographic parity testing detects bias by holding everything constant except the demographic signal (name, gender, ethnicity markers). Any systematic difference in response quality or content across demographic groups indicates bias. This is particularly important for high-stakes applications (hiring, lending, medical) where biased AI responses can cause real harm. The evaluation protocol: paired template testing with demographically diverse name sets, comparing response distributions rather than individual responses.

5. When would you use a specialized toxicity classifier (Perspective API) instead of an LLM judge to score toxicity?

Correct. Specialized classifiers vs LLM judges is a speed, cost, and interpretability tradeoff. Perspective API can evaluate 1000 responses/second for a fraction of a cent each — feasible for 100% production traffic. An LLM judge costs ~$0.003/response and takes 1-3 seconds — practical for 10-20% sampling. Classifiers return calibrated probabilities with consistent behavior. LLM judges can reason about nuanced context. Most production systems use classifiers for real-time screening and LLM judges for sampled deep evaluation.
