Module 10 · RAG Masterclass

Advanced RAG:
Query Transform, CRAG, Self-RAG

Naive RAG retrieves once with the user's raw query and hopes for the best. Advanced RAG adds intelligence at every stage: queries are transformed before retrieval, irrelevant context is compressed away, documents are retrieved at multiple granularities, and the pipeline self-corrects when retrieval quality is low.

01 · Naive RAG vs Advanced RAG

What Naive RAG Gets Wrong
and Why Each Fix Matters

Naive RAG has a single retrieval step: embed the user's raw query, search the vector store, pass top-k to the LLM. This works for simple direct questions but fails systematically on vague queries, multi-part questions, complex reasoning chains, and queries that are semantically far from the documents that answer them. Advanced RAG adds targeted fixes at each failure point.

Naive RAG failure modes and their targeted fixes

Query too vague: "Tell me about the refund policy"

Retrieves documents about all aspects of refunds. LLM receives 5 loosely related chunks, no specific answer.

Fix: Query rewriting — decompose into specific sub-questions before retrieval

Query semantically distant from documents

Query: "What happens if I want to leave?" → documents say "cancellation" not "leave". Dense retrieval fails without overlap.

Fix: HyDE — generate a hypothetical document first, embed that, search with the richer embedding

Retrieved chunk is 80% irrelevant noise

A 512-token chunk contains 400 tokens about billing history and 100 tokens about the refund policy. LLM gets confused by the surrounding noise.

Fix: Contextual compression — extract only the relevant sentences before passing to LLM

Relevant snippet but no surrounding context

Small chunk: "non-refundable after 30 days" — user needs the full policy clause with exceptions to understand the answer.

Fix: Parent-child chunking — retrieve small, read the large parent document

Retrieval quality is unknown at inference time

For 20% of queries, retrieval returns irrelevant documents. LLM hallucinates. No mechanism to detect or recover.

Fix: CRAG — self-evaluate retrieval relevance, fall back to web search if below threshold

02 · Query Transformation

Improving the Query Before
Retrieval Even Begins

The user's raw query is often a poor retrieval signal. It may be vague, ambiguous, expressed in different vocabulary from your documents, or require multi-step reasoning that a single embedding cannot capture. Query transformation techniques preprocess the query to maximize retrieval effectiveness.

Query Rewriting

Rewrite the user's query into a more specific, search-optimized form. Handles vague queries, typos, and colloquial language that the embedding model might not handle well.

User: "how do i get my money back"
Rewritten: "What is the refund process and eligibility criteria for annual subscription cancellation?"
# Prompt: "Rewrite this question to be more specific and search-optimized for a policy knowledge base: {question}"

HyDE — Hypothetical Document Embeddings

Ask the LLM to generate a hypothetical ideal answer document, then embed THAT document for retrieval. The hypothesis is richer and more domain-specific than the raw query, producing a better search vector even if the hypothesis content is wrong.

Query: "Can I get a refund after 45 days?"
HyDE generates: "Annual subscription refunds are not available after the 30-day trial period has ended. Users who purchased more than 30 days ago are not eligible for refunds per section 4.2 of the Terms of Service..."
# Embed THIS hypothesis, not the raw query. Dense retrieval now uses a document-like vector.

Step-Back Prompting

Generate a more general "step-back" question first, retrieve for that, then answer the specific question. Best for queries that assume specific context the retriever might not have indexed directly.

Specific: "Is my Teams plan refundable if I downgrade to Starter?"
Step-back: "What is Marevlo's general policy for plan downgrades and refunds?"
# Retrieve for both. The general question often surfaces the policy document that contains the answer.

Multi-Query Retrieval

Generate 3-5 different phrasings of the same question, retrieve for each, then merge and deduplicate results. Catches the relevant documents regardless of which phrasing happens to match the index vocabulary.

Original: "Can I get a refund?"
Generated: ["What is the refund policy?", "How do I cancel and get money back?", "Are subscriptions refundable?", "What are the cancellation terms?"]
# Retrieve for all 5, merge results with RRF, deduplicate. Higher recall than single query.
03 · Contextual Compression

Extract Only the Relevant
Sentences Before Passing to LLM

A retrieved 512-token chunk may contain only 80 tokens actually relevant to the query. Passing the full chunk wastes context budget, adds noise, and can confuse the LLM. Contextual compression extracts or scores individual sentences within each retrieved document relative to the query.

Before and after contextual compression

Without compression (512 tokens passed)

Marevlo was founded in 2021 with a mission to make ML education accessible... [100 tokens of company history]
Our subscription plans include Starter, Teams, and Enterprise... [80 tokens of plan descriptions]
Annual subscriptions are non-refundable after the 30-day trial period. Refund requests must be submitted via support@marevlo.com. [40 tokens — the actual answer]
Payment methods accepted include Stripe, PayPal... [80 tokens of payment info]

After compression (40 tokens passed)

Annual subscriptions are non-refundable after the 30-day trial period. Refund requests must be submitted via support@marevlo.com.
✓

88% token reduction. LLM receives only the signal, no noise. Citation accuracy improves because the compressed chunk is entirely relevant.

LLM-based compression

Ask an LLM to extract only the relevant sentences:

System: "Extract sentences from the document that are relevant to answering the question. If none are relevant, output 'IRRELEVANT'."
User: "Question: {q}\nDocument: {doc}"

Embedding-based compression

Score each sentence against the query by cosine similarity, keep only sentences above a threshold:

q_vec = embed(query)
for sent in sentences(doc):
  sim = cosine(embed(sent), q_vec)
  if sim > 0.65: keep(sent)
# Fast, no LLM call. Less accurate than LLM but 100x cheaper.
04 · Parent-Child Chunking

Retrieve Small, Read Large:
Precision Without Loss of Context

Small chunks (~100 tokens) are more precise to retrieve: they contain one focused idea, so the embedding captures one meaning, and retrieval precision is high. But small chunks often lack the surrounding context the LLM needs to generate a complete answer. Parent-child chunking gives you both: retrieve at small granularity, then expand to the larger parent document for generation.

How parent-child indexing works

Indexing time: Split each document into small child chunks (100-200 tokens each). Also store larger parent chunks (500-800 tokens) that group related children. Index ONLY child chunks in the vector store. Store parent chunks in a document store keyed by parent_id.
↓
Query time: Retrieve top-k child chunks by dense/hybrid search. For each retrieved child, look up its parent_id. Fetch the full parent chunk from the document store. Pass the parent chunks (not the children) to the LLM.

Without parent-child

Child chunk retrieved: "non-refundable after 30 days."
LLM gets 15-token chunk with no context about what subscription type, what the exceptions are, or how to apply for a refund within 30 days.

With parent-child

Child retrieved: "non-refundable after 30 days." → parent fetched: full 500-token refund policy section with all conditions, exceptions, and the support email. LLM has everything needed for a complete answer.

💡

Configuring the size ratio

Typical config: child = 150 tokens with 50-token overlap, parent = 600 tokens. The child must be small enough that its embedding captures one focused idea (less than 200 tokens is a good rule). The parent must be large enough to provide full context for the LLM but small enough to fit multiple parents in the context window (500-1000 tokens works well).

05 · Metadata Filtering

Pre-Filter With Structured Fields
Before Dense Search

Dense retrieval searches the entire corpus for every query. But many queries contain structured constraints: "Find the refund policy from version 2.3 of the terms," "Show me enterprise-plan documentation," "What changed in January 2024?" Metadata filtering applies these constraints before the vector search, dramatically reducing the search space and improving precision.

💡

Hybrid pre-filtering: combine structured and semantic

Qdrant, Weaviate, and Pinecone all support metadata filters applied before or alongside ANN search. The filter eliminates ineligible documents from the ANN graph traversal, so you get both the structured constraint AND the semantic ranking within that constrained set. Example: filter to {type: "policy", plan_tier: "enterprise"} then run dense search — you get the most semantically relevant enterprise policy documents.

06 · Corrective RAG (CRAG)

Self-Evaluate Retrieval Quality
and Fall Back When Needed

CRAG (Yan et al. 2024) adds a retrieval evaluator that assesses whether retrieved documents are actually relevant to the query before passing them to the LLM. If the evaluator decides retrieval quality is low, CRAG triggers a fallback — typically a web search — to obtain better context. This turns a passive retrieval pipeline into one that actively manages its own quality.

CRAG decision flow

Step 1: Retrieve top-k candidates from vector store (standard retrieval)
↓
Step 2: Relevance evaluator — a lightweight LLM or cross-encoder scores each (query, doc) pair for relevance. Output: CORRECT / AMBIGUOUS / INCORRECT
↓ branch
CORRECT
At least one doc is highly relevant. Use retrieved context for generation.
AMBIGUOUS
Context is partial or uncertain. Use retrieved context + refine with web search. Combine both.
INCORRECT
All retrieved docs irrelevant. Discard them. Execute web search. Use web results as context.
↓
Step 3: Strip irrelevant content from context (CORRECT/AMBIGUOUS path). Generate answer grounded in refined context.
⚠️

When CRAG is worth the added latency

CRAG adds one extra LLM call (the evaluator) per query. Worth it when: your corpus has coverage gaps and the user population asks questions that require current information, your retrieval quality varies widely across query types, or hallucination cost in your application is high (medical, legal, financial). For stable closed-domain corpora with high retrieval precision, CRAG adds overhead without benefit.

07 · Self-RAG

The LLM Decides When
to Retrieve Mid-Generation

Self-RAG (Asai et al. 2023) trains an LLM to emit special tokens during generation that trigger retrieval on demand. Instead of a fixed retrieve-then-generate pattern, the model generates text and inserts [Retrieve] tokens when it needs additional context. This allows selective retrieval: simple questions are answered from parametric memory alone; complex or uncertain parts trigger targeted retrieval.

Self-RAG generation example

User: What is Marevlo's refund policy and how does it compare to industry standards?

Model: Marevlo's refund policy governs when users can receive refunds on their subscriptions. [Retrieve] → system retrieves: "Annual subscriptions are non-refundable after 30 days"
Annual subscriptions are non-refundable after the 30-day trial period [Retrieved Doc 1]. Monthly subscriptions may be cancelled at any time. [IsRel: Relevant][IsSup: Fully supported]
This 30-day window is shorter than many SaaS competitors. [Retrieve] → retrieves: "Industry average trial period is 14-30 days per SaaS survey 2024"
The industry average trial period ranges from 14 to 30 days [Retrieved Doc 2], placing Marevlo's policy at the upper end of standard practice.[IsRel: Relevant][IsSup: Partially supported]

[Retrieve]

Emitted when the model is uncertain or needs factual grounding. Triggers retrieval of relevant documents at that generation step.

[IsRel]

After retrieval, scores whether the retrieved passage is relevant to the current generation context. Relevant / Irrelevant.

[IsSup]

After generation, scores whether the generated claim is supported by the retrieved passage. Fully / Partially / Not supported.

💡

Self-RAG in practice without special-trained models

Full Self-RAG requires a specially fine-tuned model that generates the reflection tokens. A practical approximation with any LLM: prompt it to think step by step and identify which claims need factual grounding ("I need to retrieve X"), then route those claims to retrieval mid-generation using a LangGraph agentic loop. CRAG is usually easier to implement and catches the most common retrieval failure mode.

08 · Production Architecture

All Patterns Combined:
A Full Advanced RAG Pipeline

The advanced RAG patterns compose together. A production system might use all of them simultaneously, each handling a different failure mode. Here is how they combine in a full pipeline.

User query: Raw user question arrives
↓
Query transformation: Multi-query: generate 3 phrasings. HyDE: generate hypothetical answer. Step-back: generate general version. Run all in parallel.
↓
Metadata pre-filter: Parse structured constraints (plan tier, date range, document type) from query. Apply as Qdrant/Weaviate filter before ANN search.
↓
Hybrid retrieval + RRF: BM25 + dense retrieval for each transformed query. RRF to merge all result lists. Top-100 candidates.
↓
Cross-encoder reranking: Score all 100 (query, doc) pairs. Re-rank to top-20.
↓
CRAG evaluation: Score top-5 for relevance. If INCORRECT: trigger web search fallback. If AMBIGUOUS: merge web results.
↓
Contextual compression: Extract only relevant sentences from each of the top-5 documents. Deduplicate.
↓
Parent expansion: For each compressed child chunk, fetch its parent document from the document store.
↓
LLM generation: Pass parent chunks (best-first) + grounded system prompt to LLM. Stream response.
09 · Production Code

HyDE, Multi-Query, and
Parent-Child with LangChain

advanced_rag.py

from langchain_anthropic import ChatAnthropic
from langchain_core.prompts import ChatPromptTemplate
from langchain.retrievers import MultiQueryRetriever, ParentDocumentRetriever
from langchain.retrievers.multi_query import MultiQueryRetriever
from langchain.storage import InMemoryStore
from langchain_community.vectorstores import Qdrant
from langchain_community.embeddings import HuggingFaceEmbeddings
from langchain.text_splitter import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter
from langchain.schema import Document

# ── HyDE: Hypothetical Document Embeddings ────────────────────
def hyde_retrieve(query: str, retriever, llm) -> list:
    hyde_prompt = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([(
        "human",
        "Generate a short hypothetical document that would answer this question: {question}"
    )])
    hypothesis = (hyde_prompt | llm).invoke({"question": query}).content
    # Retrieve using the hypothesis (richer embedding than raw query)
    return retriever.invoke(hypothesis)

# ── Multi-Query Retriever ─────────────────────────────────────
embeddings = HuggingFaceEmbeddings(model_name="BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5",
    encode_kwargs={"normalize_embeddings": True},
    query_instruction="Represent this sentence for searching relevant passages: ")
vectorstore = Qdrant.from_documents(
    docs, embeddings, location=":memory:", collection_name="docs")
base_retriever = vectorstore.as_retriever(search_kwargs={"k": 10})
llm = ChatAnthropic(model="claude-sonnet-4-20250514", temperature=0)

# Generates 3 query variants + retrieves + deduplicates automatically
multi_query_retriever = MultiQueryRetriever.from_llm(
    retriever=base_retriever, llm=llm)

# ── Parent-Child (ParentDocumentRetriever) ────────────────────
child_splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(chunk_size=200, chunk_overlap=50)
parent_splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(chunk_size=800, chunk_overlap=100)
docstore = InMemoryStore()

parent_retriever = ParentDocumentRetriever(
    vectorstore=vectorstore,
    docstore=docstore,
    child_splitter=child_splitter,
    parent_splitter=parent_splitter,  # omit to use full docs as parents
    k=4  # return 4 parent documents
)
parent_retriever.add_documents(raw_docs)
# Retrieves small child chunks but returns their large parent contexts
results = parent_retriever.invoke("refund policy after 30 days")

# ── CRAG relevance evaluator ─────────────────────────────────
def crag_evaluate(query: str, docs: list, llm) -> str:
    eval_prompt = ChatPromptTemplate.from_messages([(
        "human",
        "Rate retrieval quality: CORRECT, AMBIGUOUS, or INCORRECT.
Query: {query}
Top retrieved document: {doc}
Output one word only."
    )])
    verdict = (eval_prompt | llm).invoke({
        "query": query,
        "doc": docs[0].page_content[:500]
    }).content.strip().upper()
    return verdict  # CORRECT / AMBIGUOUS / INCORRECT
10 · Assessment

Module 10 Quiz

1. A user asks: "Tell me about Marevlo." Your retriever returns 5 documents covering 5 different topics (pricing, refunds, features, support, team). The LLM's answer is unfocused. Which Advanced RAG technique most directly fixes this?

Correct. Vague queries produce vague results because the embedding tries to represent everything at once. Multi-query decomposition breaks the question into focused sub-questions, each of which retrieves a specific document set. The LLM then has structured, targeted evidence to work from rather than 5 loosely related chunks.

2. HyDE embeds a hypothetical answer document instead of the raw query. Why does this improve retrieval over embedding the query directly?

Correct. HyDE works because of vocabulary alignment: user queries are informal, documents are formal. The LLM generates a hypothesis in document-like language (the right vocabulary, domain terms, structure), which embeds close to real documents in the vector space. The hypothesis being factually wrong does not matter for retrieval — only the embedding similarity to real documents matters.

3. You implement parent-child chunking with child_size=200, parent_size=800. A child chunk "non-refundable after 30 days" gets retrieved. What does the LLM actually receive as context?

Correct. This is the core mechanic: retrieve small (child) for precision, read large (parent) for context. The child's small, focused embedding enables precise retrieval. But the LLM receives the parent, which has the surrounding context needed to generate a complete answer. Without parent expansion, the LLM sees a decontextualized fragment.

4. CRAG evaluates retrieval and outputs INCORRECT for a query. What happens next in the pipeline?

Correct. CRAG's INCORRECT branch discards all retrieved documents (to prevent hallucination from irrelevant context) and falls back to web search. The web results are then treated as the RAG context for generation. This is the key value of CRAG: it turns retrieval failure from a silent hallucination risk into a handled, recoverable case.

5. Contextual compression reduces a 512-token chunk to 80 tokens before passing to the LLM. What are the two main benefits besides token cost reduction?

Correct. Two key benefits beyond cost: (1) signal-to-noise ratio — removing irrelevant context reduces the risk of the LLM being distracted by unrelated information (lost-in-the-middle phenomenon applies within a document too); (2) citation quality — a fully compressed chunk is 100% relevant, so any claim the LLM makes from it has a clear [Doc N] reference, making hallucination detection more reliable.
