01 · Limitation #1

Hallucination: When LLMs Confidently Lie

An LLM's job is to predict the statistically most plausible next token. It has no concept of "I don't know" baked in at the architecture level. When it encounters a question outside its training, it doesn't stop — it generates the most plausible-sounding continuation. That continuation may be entirely false.

76%
of LLM responses contain at least one factual error in domain-specific Q&A (Stanford HAI, 2024)
$75B
estimated annual cost of AI hallucinations to enterprises (Gartner, 2024)
~92%
reduction in hallucination rate when using RAG with good retrieval (various benchmarks)

Real hallucination examples — the model sounds certain

👤
What are the terms of the NDA I signed with Infosys in 2023?
AI
Based on standard Infosys NDAs, your 2023 agreement likely includes a 5-year confidentiality period, covers all IP developed during your engagement, and includes a ₹50 lakh penalty clause for breach. ⚠️ Every detail here was invented. The model has never seen your NDA. It generated plausible NDA language. This could lead to serious legal decisions based on false information.
👤
Who is the current CTO of our company, TechNova Pvt. Ltd.?
AI
The CTO of TechNova Pvt. Ltd. is Rajesh Sharma, who joined the company in 2019 after a career at Wipro. ⚠️ There is no TechNova Pvt. Ltd. in training data. "Rajesh Sharma" is a completely fabricated name. The model invented a plausible Indian tech executive biography.
🧠

Why hallucination is not a "bug" — it's how LLMs work

Transformers are next-token predictors. They are trained to maximize the probability of the training data, not to be truthful. "Truth" is not a concept in the loss function. The model produces the most statistically plausible text — which, for unknown facts, is a confident-sounding fabrication. RAG fixes this by giving the model real facts to work from.

Detecting hallucination — basic faithfulness check

Python
from openai import OpenAI

client = OpenAI()

def faithfulness_check(answer: str, context: str) -> dict:
    """
    Ask an LLM to judge whether the answer is grounded in the context.
    This is the simplest form of RAG evaluation.
    In production, use RAGAS or DeepEval for this.
    """
    prompt = f"""You are an objective judge. Given the Context and the Answer below,
determine if every factual claim in the Answer is supported by the Context.

Context:
{context}

Answer:
{answer}

Respond with JSON:
{{
  "faithful": true/false,
  "unsupported_claims": ["list any claims not in context"],
  "reasoning": "brief explanation"
}}"""

    resp = client.chat.completions.create(
        model="gpt-4o-mini",
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content": prompt}],
        response_format={"type": "json_object"},
        temperature=0
    )
    import json
    return json.loads(resp.choices[0].message.content)

# Example: RAG answer should be faithful; pure LLM answer should not
context = "Marevlo annual subscriptions cancelled after 30 days are non-refundable."

rag_answer = "Annual subscriptions cancelled after 30 days are non-refundable."
llm_answer = "Marevlo offers a 50% refund for cancellations within 60 days."

print(faithfulness_check(rag_answer, context))
# {'faithful': True, 'unsupported_claims': [], 'reasoning': 'Directly stated.'}

print(faithfulness_check(llm_answer, context))
# {'faithful': False, 'unsupported_claims': ['50% refund', '60 day window'], ...}
02 · Limitation #2

Stale Knowledge: The Frozen Clock

Every LLM has a training cutoff date. After that date, the world changes but the model does not. For enterprise applications — where laws change, products are updated, prices shift, people change roles — this is not an edge case. It is a constant problem.

🗓️ Major model cutoffs

GPT-4 Turbo: Jan 2024
Claude 3.5 Sonnet: Apr 2024
Gemini 1.5 Pro: Nov 2023
Llama 3.1: Dec 2023

Anything after these dates: the model either doesn't know or hallucinates.

📉 Real-world staleness examples

→"What's our GST rate?" — changed 3 times in 18 months
→"Who is the RBI governor?" — answered with a previous appointee
→"What's the current API pricing?" — 6 months out of date
→"Is this drug approved in India?" — approval post-cutoff
✅

How RAG solves staleness: knowledge at retrieval time, not training time

With RAG, your vector database is your source of truth — not the model's weights. Update a document in your database and every future query automatically gets the new information. The model's training cutoff becomes irrelevant because you're providing current facts in the prompt.

03 · Limitation #3

Context Window: You Can't Fit Everything in a Prompt

Modern LLMs have context windows of 128K to 1M tokens. That sounds enormous until you consider: a single legal contract is 50K tokens, a medium-sized internal wiki is 10M tokens, a year of customer support tickets is 100M tokens. Even 1M tokens cannot hold a real enterprise knowledge base.

⚠️ Context window exceeded! You're trying to fit more than 128K tokens. The model will either truncate documents (losing information) or throw an error.
💡

Why RAG beats "just use a bigger context window"

Research from Google and Stanford shows that LLMs suffer from "lost in the middle" — they pay much less attention to information in the middle of a long context. RAG retrieves only the 3–10 most relevant chunks (typically 1–5K tokens), keeping the context focused and attention high. Quality beats quantity.

04 · Limitation #4

Private Data: The Model Never Saw Your World

No LLM was trained on your company's internal documents, your proprietary databases, your customer conversations, or your codebase. This is not a limitation that will be fixed by bigger models — it's a fundamental architectural reality. Fine-tuning is prohibitively expensive and still gives you stale knowledge. RAG is the standard answer.

📄 Documents

Internal wikis, HR policies, product specs, legal agreements, SOP documents, pitch decks

🗄️ Databases

Customer records, inventory systems, pricing tables, ERP data, CRM entries

💬 Conversations

📊 Reports

💻 Code

Building a private knowledge base — production-ready skeleton

Python
from pathlib import Path
from langchain_community.document_loaders import (
    PyPDFLoader,         # PDFs
    UnstructuredWordDocumentLoader,  # .docx
    NotionDirectoryLoader,  # Notion export
    ConfluenceLoader,     # Confluence wiki
    SlackDirectoryLoader, # Slack export
)
from langchain_openai import OpenAIEmbeddings
from langchain_community.vectorstores import Chroma
from langchain.text_splitter import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter

def build_private_knowledge_base(
    docs_path: str,
    persist_dir: str = "./chroma_db"
) -> Chroma:
    """
    Load ALL company documents, chunk them, embed them, and
    store in a persistent vector database.
    """
    documents = []

    # Load every PDF in the folder
    for pdf_path in Path(docs_path).glob("**/*.pdf"):
        loader = PyPDFLoader(str(pdf_path))
        docs = loader.load()
        # Add source metadata — crucial for citations
        for doc in docs:
            doc.metadata["source_type"] = "pdf"
            doc.metadata["filename"] = pdf_path.name
        documents.extend(docs)

    # Load Word documents
    for docx_path in Path(docs_path).glob("**/*.docx"):
        loader = UnstructuredWordDocumentLoader(str(docx_path))
        docs = loader.load()
        for doc in docs:
            doc.metadata["source_type"] = "word"
            doc.metadata["filename"] = docx_path.name
        documents.extend(docs)

    # Split into chunks (we'll cover chunking strategy in Module 5)
    splitter = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(
        chunk_size=1000,      # tokens per chunk
        chunk_overlap=200,   # overlap prevents boundary blindness
        separators=["\n\n", "\n", ". ", " "]
    )
    chunks = splitter.split_documents(documents)
    print(f"Created {len(chunks)} chunks from {len(documents)} pages")

    # Embed and store — Chroma persists to disk
    vectorstore = Chroma.from_documents(
        documents=chunks,
        embedding=OpenAIEmbeddings(model="text-embedding-3-small"),
        persist_directory=persist_dir
    )
    print(f"Knowledge base built and saved to {persist_dir}")
    return vectorstore

# Build once — takes minutes for a typical company wiki
kb = build_private_knowledge_base("./company_docs")

# Query instantly — takes milliseconds
results = kb.similarity_search("What is our parental leave policy?", k=3)
for r in results:
    print(f"Source: {r.metadata['filename']}")
    print(f"Content: {r.page_content[:200]}...")
05 · The Full Picture

Four Limitations, One Solution

Every failure mode we've seen has the same root cause: the model is working from a fixed, private, bounded memory. RAG externalizes that memory into a living, searchable, up-to-date database. Here's how each limitation is addressed.

Limitation Root Cause RAG Solution
Hallucination No grounding in real facts; model predicts plausible text from statistical patterns Retrieved context gives the model real facts to reason from; prompt instructs "use only context"
Stale Knowledge Knowledge frozen in model weights at training cutoff; weights can't be updated cheaply Vector DB is updated independently; new docs indexed in minutes; model gets current facts at query time
Context Limits Can't fit entire knowledge base in prompt; even large windows cause "lost in the middle" attention failures Retrieval selects only the 3–10 most relevant chunks; focused context = better attention = better answers
Private Data Company data never in training set; fine-tuning too expensive and still produces stale knowledge Any document can be indexed; no model retraining needed; knowledge base owned and controlled by you
