// Section 1

Why Spread Matters as Much as Center

The mean tells you where the data clusters. Spread tells you how reliable that center is. A delivery time with mean=3, SD=0.1 means almost every delivery is between 2.7 and 3.3 days. A delivery time with mean=3, SD=2 means deliveries range from 1 day to 5+ days. For Kartify's SLA promises, the second warehouse is a disaster even though the mean is identical.

⚡ The Core Intuition

Spread measures how much the data disagrees with the mean. If all orders arrive in exactly 3 days — no disagreement, spread = 0. If orders arrive anywhere from 1 to 7 days — high disagreement, high spread. Every spread measure is, at its core, a different way of quantifying how far typical data points are from the center.

Range & IQR — Quick Spread Measures

Kartify — Delivery Days Across 10 Orders
Data (sorted): 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 9
Range = max − min = 9 − 1 = 8 days. Problem: one late Rajasthan delivery (9 days) makes the range look huge. Range uses only 2 data points and is completely non-robust.
Q1 (25th percentile) = 2, Q3 (75th percentile) = 4 (average of 4th & 5th, and 8th & 9th values respectively)
IQR = Q3 − Q1 = 4 − 2 = 2 days. This covers the middle 50% of orders. The 9-day outlier has zero effect on IQR.
→ Kartify reports IQR=2 days in its SLA documentation — robust, outlier-resistant, and tells customers what 50% of their orders experience.
// Section 3

Variance — The Foundation

Variance is the average squared deviation from the mean. Squaring serves two purposes: (1) makes all deviations positive, and (2) penalizes large deviations more — a 4-day deviation contributes 16× more than a 1-day deviation.

Sample Variance (Bessel's Corrected)
s² = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / (n − 1)
(xᵢ − x̄)²squared deviation of each point from the mean
Σsum over all n observations
n−1Bessel's correction — makes s² unbiased estimator of σ²
unitssquared units of original data (days², ₹²)
// Section 4

Bessel's Correction — Why n−1?

💡 The Deep Reason

When you estimate the mean from the same sample you're computing variance from, you've already used one "piece of information" to calculate x̄. The residuals Σ(xᵢ−x̄) are constrained to sum to zero — so only n−1 of them are free to vary. Dividing by n−1 instead of n corrects this and makes s² an unbiased estimator of the true population variance σ²: E[s²] = σ².

Practical rule: n−1 (Bessel's) for sample statistics (when inferring about a population). n for population statistics (when you have all the data). In pandas: df.var(ddof=1) vs df.var(ddof=0).

// Section 5

Standard Deviation — Back in Original Units

Variance is in squared units (₹², days²) which is impossible to interpret directly. Standard deviation is simply the square root of variance — bringing it back to the original units.

Sample Standard Deviation
s = √s² = √[ Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² / (n−1) ]
ssame units as original data (days, ₹, kg)
68-95-99.7for normal data: ~68% within 1σ, 95% within 2σ
s=0all values are identical — zero spread

Coefficient of Variation — Comparing Across Scales

Standard deviation can't be compared across different units. Is SD=₹50 high or low? Depends on whether we're talking about ₹100 sarees or ₹50,000 laptops. The coefficient of variation (CV) normalizes SD by the mean.

Coefficient of Variation
CV = (s / x̄) × 100%
CVrelative spread — unitless, expressed as %
Kartify low-priceSD=₹50 on mean ₹100 → CV=50% (very variable)
Kartify high-priceSD=₹500 on mean ₹50,000 → CV=1% (extremely stable)
// Section 7

MAD — Robust Alternative to SD

⚡ When to Use MAD

Mean Absolute Deviation (MAD = Σ|xᵢ − median| / n) uses the median as center and absolute values instead of squaring. Result: robust to outliers. One ₹25,000 luxury order doesn't explode the MAD like it would the SD. Kartify's fraud team uses MAD to detect unusual delivery patterns without outlier contamination.

Python — Complete Spread Implementation

Python — Kartify Delivery Times: Full Spread Analysis 
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from scipy import stats

# Kartify delivery times (days) — two warehouses
mumbai   = np.array([3,3,3,3,2,3,4,3,3,3])   # consistent
kolkata  = np.array([1,5,2,6,1,4,3,5,2,1])   # unpredictable

for name, data in [('Mumbai', mumbai), ('Kolkata', kolkata)]:
    print(f"\n=== {name} ===")
    print(f"Mean:     {np.mean(data):.2f} days")
    print(f"Range:    {np.ptp(data):.0f} days")          # max - min
    print(f"IQR:      {stats.iqr(data):.2f} days")         # Q3 - Q1
    print(f"Variance: {np.var(data, ddof=1):.2f} days²")   # Bessel-corrected
    print(f"Std Dev:  {np.std(data, ddof=1):.2f} days")    # ddof=1 = sample
    cv = np.std(data, ddof=1) / np.mean(data) * 100
    print(f"CV:       {cv:.1f}%")
    mad = np.mean(np.abs(data - np.median(data)))
    print(f"MAD:      {mad:.2f} days")

# Comparing spread across different-scale products
sarees   = np.array([450,520,380,610,470])    # avg ₹486
laptops  = np.array([45000,52000,38000,61000,47000])  # avg ₹48600

print(f"\nSarees  SD=₹{np.std(sarees,ddof=1):.0f}  CV={np.std(sarees,ddof=1)/np.mean(sarees)*100:.1f}%")
print(f"Laptops SD=₹{np.std(laptops,ddof=1):.0f}  CV={np.std(laptops,ddof=1)/np.mean(laptops)*100:.1f}%")
# Laptops have higher SD in ₹ but similar CV% — relatively equal price consistency

Module 1.3 — What You've Mastered
  • Range = max−min. Simple but non-robust — one outlier can dominate it entirely.
  • IQR = Q3−Q1. Covers middle 50% of data. Robust to outliers. Kartify uses this for delivery SLA reporting.
  • Variance s² = Σ(xᵢ−x̄)²/(n−1). Average squared deviation. Bessel's n−1 makes it unbiased for σ².
  • Standard Deviation s = √s². Same units as data. Most widely reported spread measure.
  • For normal data: 68% within ±1σ, 95% within ±2σ, 99.7% within ±3σ.
  • Coefficient of Variation CV = (s/x̄)×100%. Enables spread comparison across different scales and units.
  • MAD = Σ|xᵢ−median|/n. Robust alternative to SD — unaffected by extreme outliers.
  • Python: np.std(ddof=1), np.var(ddof=1), scipy.stats.iqr(), np.ptp() for range.
