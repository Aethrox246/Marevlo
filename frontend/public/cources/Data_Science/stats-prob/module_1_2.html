Why Do We Have Three Different Averages?

The word "average" is dangerously vague. In statistics, there are at least five distinct measures of center — each answering a subtly different question about where the data clusters. The right choice depends entirely on what question you're actually trying to answer.

⚡ The Central Question

If you had to replace all values in your dataset with a single number — what number would cause the least "damage"? Different definitions of "damage" lead to different answers: least total error → mean. Least squared error → also mean. Middle rank → median. Most common → mode. There is no single "best" average — only the best average for your purpose.

🛍️ Why CEO got ₹847
Arithmetic Mean
Added all 12,000 order values and divided by 12,000. Mathematically correct but pulled up by 50 ultra-luxury orders (₹25,000+ each). The "average" customer did NOT spend ₹847 — most spent under ₹500.
🛍️ Why CFO got ₹612
Median
Sorted all 12,000 orders and took the middle value. ₹612 is what the "typical" Kartify customer actually spends. Not affected by those 50 luxury outliers. The CFO wants to know what a representative customer looks like for budgeting.
🛍️ Why Luxury Head got ₹2,340
Mode (Segment)
Looked only at their luxury segment's orders. The most frequently occurring price tier was ₹2,340. Completely valid within that segment — but she's answering a different question about a different subset of data.
✅ The Lesson
Specify the question first
Before computing any average, ask: what does this number represent? Typical customer behavior? Use median. Total revenue per order for accounting? Mean. Most popular price point for inventory? Mode. The measure must match the question.

The Arithmetic Mean

The mean is what most people mean by "average." Sum all values, divide by count. It's the balance point of the distribution — like finding where a seesaw would balance if each data point were a weight.

Arithmetic Mean
x̄ = (1/n) Σᵢ xᵢ = (x₁ + x₂ + ... + xₙ) / n
x̄"x-bar" — sample mean
nnumber of observations
Σxᵢsum of all values
Σ(xᵢ−x̄)=0deviations always cancel — mean is the balance point
Kartify Worked Example — Daily GMV (7 Days)
Data: Monday–Sunday GMV (₹ lakhs): 42, 38, 51, 47, 89, 134, 97
Sum: 42+38+51+47+89+134+97 = 498
Mean: 498 ÷ 7 = ₹71.1 lakhs/day
Interpretation: The weekend effect (₹134L Saturday, ₹97L Sunday) pulls the mean up. A typical weekday is actually ₹43–51L. The mean isn't wrong — it's the right number for "total weekly GMV / 7 days" but misleading for "what does a typical day look like."
→ Kartify's GM reports ₹71.1L/day to investors. Ops team plans around ₹47L (median). Both are correct for their purpose.
⚠️ The Mean's Fatal Weakness — Outliers

Add one ₹50,000 luxury order to a dataset of 99 orders worth ₹500 each: mean jumps from ₹500 to ₹995 — nearly double. But 99 of 100 customers still paid ₹500. The mean is non-robust — a single extreme value can drag it arbitrarily far from where the data actually clusters. This is exactly what happens with Kartify's GMV when luxury orders spike.

The Median — Robust Center

The median is the middle value when data is sorted. It's robust — one extreme outlier, no matter how extreme, can only move the median by at most one rank. This makes it the preferred average whenever data is skewed or has outliers.

Median Calculation
If n is odd: median = x₍ₙ₊₁₎/₂    If n is even: median = (x₍ₙ/₂₎ + x₍ₙ/₂₊₁₎) / 2
Step 1Sort all values ascending
Step 2Find middle position (n+1)/2
Even nAverage the two middle values
Kartify — Delivery Days Across 7 Cities
Data (unsorted): Mumbai=1, Delhi=2, Bengaluru=3, Chennai=4, Hyderabad=3, Kolkata=5, Jaipur=8
Sorted: 1, 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, 8
n = 7 (odd) → middle position = (7+1)/2 = 4th value
Median = 3 days. Even if Jaipur's 8 days becomes 30 days (due to a route disruption), the median stays 3. Mean would jump from 3.7 to 7.0.
→ Kartify's SLA promise "delivered in 3 days or less for most customers" is based on the median — not the mean that Jaipur's remote deliveries skew.
// Section 4

The Mode — Most Frequent

The mode is the value that appears most often. It's the only measure of center that works for nominal data, and it's what businesses really care about for inventory and pricing decisions.

🛍️ Kartify Inventory Planning
Mode = stock MORE of this
Last month's most ordered size: M (ordered 3,847 times). Most ordered price tier: ₹499 (ordered 5,612 times). Most returned reason: "Too small". Kartify's inventory team uses mode — not mean — to decide what to stock more of.
📊 Multiple Modes
Bimodal = Two customer segments
Kartify's order values cluster around ₹499 AND ₹2,999. Two modes — bimodal distribution. This reveals two distinct customer segments: budget buyers and premium buyers. A single mean (₹1,200) describes neither segment accurately.

Geometric & Harmonic Mean — Specialized Averages

💡 Kartify Growth Rates

Geometric mean is for rates and ratios. Kartify grew GMV by +200% in Y1, −50% in Y2, +200% in Y3. Arithmetic mean of growth rates = (200−50+200)/3 = 116.7%. But that's wrong! Start at ₹100. After Y1: ₹300. After Y2: ₹150. After Y3: ₹450. That's only +65% total over 3 years, or a geometric mean of ∛(3 × 0.5 × 3) − 1 ≈ 44.2% per year. The arithmetic mean wildly overstates real growth. Always use geometric mean for compound growth rates.

Geometric Mean — For Growth Rates & Compound Returns
G = (x₁ × x₂ × ... × xₙ)^(1/n) = exp( (1/n)Σ ln(xᵢ) )
Use for:Growth rates, investment returns, GMV growth factors
Example:CAGR of Kartify's GMV over 4 years
Note:All values must be positive
Harmonic Mean — For Rates and Speeds
H = n / Σ(1/xᵢ)
Use for:Speeds, rates, price-per-unit averaging
Kartify:Average delivery speed across routes (km/hour)
ML use:F1 Score = harmonic mean of precision & recall
// Section 7

Python: Computing All Measures on Kartify Data

Python — Kartify Order Values: Mean, Median, Mode & More 
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from scipy import stats
from scipy.stats.mstats import gmean

# Kartify last 14 days order values (₹)
orders = np.array([299,499,499,649,799,499,1299,2499,
                   399,499,899,1599,499,24999])  # last = luxury outlier

# ── Arithmetic mean ──────────────────────────────────
mean = np.mean(orders)
print(f"Mean:   ₹{mean:.0f}")     # ₹2,637 (luxury outlier pulls it up!)

# ── Median (robust) ──────────────────────────────────
median = np.median(orders)
print(f"Median: ₹{median:.0f}")   # ₹649 (not affected by ₹24,999)

# ── Mode ─────────────────────────────────────────────
mode = stats.mode(orders, keepdims=True)
print(f"Mode:   ₹{mode.mode[0]} (appears {mode.count[0]} times)")  # ₹499

# ── Trimmed mean (remove top/bottom 10%) ──────────
trimmed = stats.trim_mean(orders, proportiontocut=0.1)
print(f"Trimmed mean (10%): ₹{trimmed:.0f}")  # robust mean

# ── Geometric mean (for growth rates) ────────────
# Kartify quarterly GMV growth factors: 1.3, 0.9, 1.5, 1.2 (30%, -10%, 50%, 20%)
growth_factors = np.array([1.3, 0.9, 1.5, 1.2])
geo_mean = gmean(growth_factors)
print(f"Quarterly CAGR: {(geo_mean-1)*100:.1f}%")   # correct compound rate
print(f"Arithmetic mean rate: {(np.mean(growth_factors)-1)*100:.1f}%")  # overstates!

# ── pandas describe() — all in one ───────────────
s = pd.Series(orders, name='order_value_inr')
print(s.describe().round(0))
# count   14.0   mean  2637.0   std  6488.0
# min    299.0   25%   449.0    50%   649.0  ← median
# 75%   1124.0   max  24999.0

Mean, Median & Skewness — Pearson's Rule

The relative positions of mean, median, and mode reveal the shape of the distribution. This is Pearson's empirical rule — and it lets you diagnose skewness just from summary statistics, without plotting.

Skewness Direction — How Mean, Median, Mode Line Up Click a scenario to see Kartify examples
💡 Pearson's Empirical Rule

Right-skewed (Kartify GMV, income, delivery delays): Mode < Median < Mean. The long right tail pulls the mean rightward. Report median for "typical," mean for totals.
Left-skewed (Kartify customer ratings: most give 4–5 stars, few give 1): Mean < Median < Mode. Mean understates typical satisfaction.
Symmetric: Mean ≈ Median ≈ Mode. The three are interchangeable. Use mean.

Module 1.2 — What You've Mastered
  • Mean = balance point of distribution. Property: Σ(xᵢ−x̄) = 0. Non-robust — pulled by outliers.
  • Median = middle value of sorted data. Robust to outliers. Best for skewed data (Kartify GMV, income).
  • Mode = most frequent value. Only valid measure for nominal data. A dataset can be bimodal/multimodal.
  • Right-skewed: Mode < Median < Mean (Kartify GMV, income). Left-skewed: Mean < Median < Mode.
  • Geometric mean for compound growth rates and ratios. Kartify's CAGR uses geometric mean, not arithmetic.
  • Harmonic mean for speeds and rates. F1 Score = harmonic mean of precision & recall.
  • Python: np.mean(), np.median(), scipy.stats.mode(), scipy.stats.trim_mean(), scipy.stats.mstats.gmean()
  • pandas .describe() gives count, mean, std, min, 25%, 50% (median), 75%, max in one line.
